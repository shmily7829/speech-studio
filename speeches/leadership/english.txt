Recently, I met with my manager. I thought we would just talk about a project. Instead, he asked me to lead our project team. He believed I was ready. I felt nervous, but I wanted to give it a try.

To prepare for this speech, I took a leadership style test. My top style was democratic leadership. Leaders with this style usually listen to people’s ideas before making a decision.

The test helped me learn more about myself. It also made me think about my manager’s leadership style.

I think he is a coaching leader. When I have a technical problem, he doesn’t just give me the answer. He first asks what I think and lets me try. When I get stuck and ask for help, he offers advice. This helps me learn and grow, and I hope to become a leader like him.

But then I thought about my own style. At work, I listen to other people’s ideas, but I still do things my way.

When someone has a problem, I usually give them my solution. At least to me, it feels helpful and fast.

But when they don’t understand my idea or don’t want to try it, I get upset, and the problem is still there.

When I talked to my manager about this, he told me it’s hard to get people to do something they don’t want to do. He told me, “What matters is solving the problem.”

His advice made me think. Was I focused on our goal? Or did I care too much about doing things my way?

My way is one choice. Other people’s ideas can work too. They might even work better.

With that in mind, there are three things I want to practice.

First, set a clear goal.

Before we start, I’ll confirm the goal and the deadline with the team. I can think of a solution, but I’ll keep it to myself at first. That gives others a chance to think before they hear my answer.

Second, give people room to work.

Once the goal is clear, I’ll let each person plan their own work. I’ll give them time to try and learn before I step in.

Third, check the results and point out anything missing.

I’ll check whether we’ve met the goal. If I notice something they may have missed, I’ll point it out and ask them to check it. That way, we can stay on track while they take responsibility for their work.

Letting people try their own ideas helps the team grow and find new ways to do things. It also gives me a chance to keep an open mind and learn from their ideas and experience.

For me, seeing the bigger picture means spending less time on every detail and giving people more room. This helps me step back and focus on solving bigger problems.

That’s how I hope to become a better leader.

Thank you.
